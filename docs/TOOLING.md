# Cutting tool survey for CNC and robotic machining of stone and wood

Date: 2026-11-12.

Purpose: the source for the lithocam tool library. Every row here is a tool in `stone_tool_library()` or `wood_tool_library()` (`include/lithocam/tools.hpp`), with the same id (S1..S25, W1..W35, plus variants S4b, W23b, W27b). Each tool's `source` field names its row. Sizes a source states are used as given; other sizes are placeholders, and the tool's name says "example size".

Scope: a light web survey of about 35 standard searches. No PDFs were downloaded, no sign-ins were used, no paywalls were crossed. Sources are linked; no source text is reproduced.

Drop-cutter coverage today: 20 of the 63 library tools map onto the kernel's flat, ball, bull and cone cutters (exact, or conservative for annular tools). The envelope classes listed at the end are the roadmap for the rest.

## How to read this file

- 60 rows: 25 stone, 35 wood. Row IDs are S1..S25 and W1..W35.
- Column "lithocam" says whether the row maps onto an existing lithocam kind
  (flat end mill, ball end mill, bull-nose mill, face mill, chamfer mill, V-bit engraver,
  drill, core drill, tap, thread mill, profile wheel, disc saw, diamond wire) with extra
  parameters, or needs a NEW kind.
- 32 rows map to an existing kind. 28 rows need a new kind or envelope class.
- Column "Envelope" uses the codes in the legend below.
- Sizes appear only when a source states them. Most size figures come from vendor
  listings. Several vendor pages returned HTTP 403 to a direct fetch; for those the figure
  comes from the search-engine snippet of that page. Treat vendor numbers as typical, not
  as standards.

### Envelope legend

Body of revolution about the spindle axis (R-), with the lower-surface shape:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| R-FLAT | cylinder, flat lower surface (existing) |
| R-BALL | ball lower surface (existing) |
| R-TORUS | bull nose (existing) |
| R-CONE | cone (existing) |
| R-TBALL | ball tip blended into a cone flank (tapered ball nose) |
| R-ANNULUS | hollow ring; lower surface is a flat annulus; centre leaves a core or plug |
| R-PROFILE-SIDE | arbitrary generatrix acting on the side (edge-forming); lower surface flat or open |
| R-PROFILE-TIP | arbitrary generatrix acting at the tip (burrs, flame, inverted cone) |
| R-UNDERCUT | head wider than the neck above it (dovetail, T-slot, keyhole) |
| R-STEP | stepped combination (pilot drill + cone + counterbore collar) |
| R-COMPLIANT | flexible pad or brush; contact shape depends on force |

Not a body of revolution about the spindle axis:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| H-CYL | cylinder rotating about an axis normal to the feed and parallel to the surface (planer heads, rollers) |
| DISC | thin saw disc (existing disc saw) |
| WIRE / WIRE-ARRAY | single diamond wire (existing) / many parallel wires |
| BLADE-RECIP | straight blade(s) in reciprocating frame |
| BLADE-BAND | continuous band blade |
| CHAIN | chain with inserts running round a bar |
| KNIFE | oriented blade, drag or oscillating, tangential axis |
| IMPACT-POINT | reciprocating or rotating array of hard points |
| IMPACT-EDGE | reciprocating or hand-like edge (chisel, gouge) |
| WEDGE | static wedge force in a drilled hole |
| JET | abrasive waterjet stream; depth set by energy and speed, not by geometry |
| THERMAL | flame; non-contact surface spalling |
| HOLDER | tool holder that re-orients another cutter (aggregate head) |

## How tool choice differs between stone and wood

- **Cutting mechanism.** Stone tools are mostly abrasive: diamond grit held in a metal or
  resin matrix. Tönshoff and co-authors describe metal-bonded diamond tools as the main cost
  factor in stone processing
  ([Key Eng. Mater. 250](https://www.scientific.net/KEM.250.103)). Wood tools are
  defined-edge carbide, HSS or PCD knives that shear a chip
  ([Wei et al. 2018 review, BioResources](https://bioresources.cnr.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/BioRes_13_1_2139_Wei_LXTMZWW_Research_Progress_Machining_Mechanism_Milling_Wood_11901.pdf)).
- **Wear.** In stone the binder erodes against swarf and the grit is lost; binder wear
  differs clearly between marble and granite, so bond choice depends on the stone class
  ([Ternero et al. 2021, Materials 14:3988](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8306793)).
  In wood the edge recedes and chips; silica and extractives in the wood or board drive
  the wear, and cement-bonded board damaged HSS edges severely
  ([Darmawan et al. 2012](https://bioresources.cnr.ncsu.edu/BioRes_07/BioRes_07_4_4715_Darmawan_RNM_Extractives_Abrasives_Wood_Wear_Tools_3002.pdf)).
- **Coolant.** Stone CNC router tools mostly need water cooling for performance and diamond
  life ([Dynamic Stone Tools, router bits](https://dynamicstonetools.com/collections/router-bits?page=2)).
  A 2026 marble-milling study reports that without cooling the diamond coating and tool
  material burn, leading to rapid wear
  ([SAGE journal paper, DOI 10.1177/17515831261437884](https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.1177/17515831261437884)).
  None of the wood sources in this survey use coolant. The flamed and bush-hammered stone
  finishes have no wood counterpart in the sources.
- **Feeds.** Wood routing is set by chip load per tooth: Amana gives 0.005 to 0.007 in per
  tooth for a 1/4 in 2-flute spiral at 18,000 rpm, which gives 180 to 250 in/min
  ([Amana solid-wood plunge chart](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/1558/5651/files/Solid-Carbide-Spiral-Plunge-Solid-Wood-v5.pdf)).
  A stone roundover wheel is listed at 200 to 250 mm/min at 4,500 to 5,000 rpm
  ([APEXX roundover wheel](https://dynamicstonetools.odoo.com/tr/shop/22701-02003-apexx-cnc-edge-roundover-wheel-ø115-x-ø50-x-r30-69451)).
  The wood feed (about 4,570 mm/min at the low end) is roughly 18 times the stone feed in
  these two vendor listings. Different tool sizes and operations; compare with care.
- **Surface finish.** Stone finish comes from a grit sequence, for example 50 to 3000 grit
  with resin pads after metal-bond grinding
  ([DeFusco](https://www.defusco.com/diamond-polishing-pads-for-stone/)). Wood finish
  comes from cutting geometry: at least about 20 knife marks per inch, better toward 30,
  with 20 to 25 deg rake at slower feeds
  ([Stewart, FPL research note](https://www.woodweb.com/knowledge_base/fpl_pdfs/rn_nc196.pdf)).
- **Material-driven selection.** Stone tool choice follows stone class: sintered for granite,
  electroplated for marble in wire beads
  ([Toolwits](https://www.toolwits.com/info/how-to-choose-the-right-diamond-wire-103160008.html)).
  Wood tool choice follows grain and panel faces: helix direction is picked to protect
  the top, bottom, or both faces
  ([SpeTool](https://spetools.com/blogs/tool-selection-guide/up-cut-down-cut-compression-router-bits-guide)),
  and PCD replaces carbide on abrasive panels
  ([ToolsToday](https://toolstoday.com/learn/pcd-router-bits-when-are-they-worth-the-investment)).

## Envelope classes lithocam would need beyond flat, ball, torus, cone

Derived from Tables A and B. This is a design suggestion, not a source claim.

1. R-TBALL (W8). Drop-cutter needs a sphere-plus-cone lower surface.
2. R-ANNULUS (S3, S7, W26). Lower surface is a ring; the centre leaves material.
3. R-PROFILE-TIP (S13). Arbitrary lower-surface curve f(d).
4. R-UNDERCUT (W11 to W13). Drop-cutter sees a flat bottom; collision and gouge checks must
   use the full generatrix, and entry must come from the side for T-slot bits.
5. R-STEP (W25). Piecewise generatrix.
6. R-COMPLIANT (S8, S10, S11). Contact patch depends on normal force; needs force or
   offset model, not rigid geometry.
7. H-CYL (S6 rollers, W28, W29). Horizontal-axis peripheral cutter.
8. Non-rotational kinds: BLADE-RECIP array (S16), WIRE-ARRAY (S18), CHAIN (S19, W32, W34),
   BLADE-BAND (W33), KNIFE (W31), IMPACT-POINT (S21, S22), IMPACT-EDGE (S23, W35),
   WEDGE (S24), JET (S20), THERMAL (S25).
9. HOLDER transform (W30). An aggregate head offsets and rotates a mounted tool.

## Caveats

- Most geometry figures come from vendor listings. Peer-reviewed or standards sources were
  found for wear, waterjet, chain saws, robotic carving, and wood machining, not for
  tool dimensions.
- Several vendor pages (Stonegate, Amastone, Diamond Tool Store) returned HTTP 403 to a
  direct fetch. Figures attributed to them come from search-engine snippets of those pages.
- W6 (chipbreaker) geometry is not confirmed beyond a catalogue listing.
- No ISO or ANSI standard for stone tool dimensions turned up in this light survey.
- Not covered: robotic sanding discs and flap wheels for wood, hollow-chisel mortisers,
  stone lathe turning tools, sandblasting. Each needs a separate search.
